two-button menu state (closeMenu), and in waitButton / waitButtonReady (return -1 with leaveMenu, as a long MENU).

WHAT AND WHY
Paul, in Tacheo Testers, about his 1-button and 2-button units:
  "On the 1 button unit, at Ready, quick tap starts timer for station shot, long press for LRUD. I prefer this
   method as holding the button for a shot introduces movement from tense muscles. The 2 button unit is the
   opposite ... They need to function the same regardless, as it is confusing as they now function."
Sully: "Yep, do all fixes."

Until now the tap-countdown (1.16.77) was on the one-button colour builds only (sap5xc, cavec, sap6c). The
two-button builds kept hold = shot, tap = LRUD. From 1.17.7 every build works the same way:

  READY      tap SHOT              = the countdown shot. Settings > Shot timer (1.5 - 5 s) counts from the
                                     let-go; hands off while the bar fills; beep = shot
             SHOT held 0.5 s       = an LRUD, taken at the half-second mark
             any press during the countdown (SHOT or MENU) calls it off (a warning beep). That press is used
                                     up: no new countdown, no menu
             MENU                  = the menu (as before)
             MENU held             = Stations (as before)
             SHOT + MENU together  = nothing (they are the 3-s switch-off)
  RESULT     tap SHOT              = back to READY (the next tap is the countdown)
             SHOT held 0.5 s       = an LRUD
             MENU / MENU held      = the menu / Stations (as before)
  Tools' hold-for-a-shot pages (Rigging, the tutorial's practice): tap = the countdown, as on READY.
  The tutorial's two-button pages say so ("Aim the dot. Tap 1: hands off while the bar fills"; "Hold 1 half
  a second: LRUD shot"). The Settings row reads "Shot timer" on every build.

HOW
- TAP_ANY (new, 1 on every build) compiles the countdown code; TAP_SHOT still marks the one-button colour
  builds (their 2-tap menu). tapShot() is true on every build. On the OLED builds soloKeys() is false and the
  tap tracking's names (soloTapUpAt, SOLO_LRUD_MS, soloForget) exist. The OLED's SHOT click is decided at
  its let-go, so there is no 0.4-s wait.
- READY and a result: a SHOT + MENU press marks both buttons ignored, so the switch-off combination never
  becomes an LRUD or a menu. MENU during the countdown calls it off. MENU held = Stations is kept in the

released 2026-11-10
Tacheo firmware 1.17.0 - cave (Tacheo 1, OLED, 2 buttons) and cavec (Tacheo 1, colour screen, 1 button) - 10 Nov 2026

WHAT AND WHY
Paul, 10 Nov 2026, both units on 1.16.99: the 2-button's READY laser is "back to turning off the laser at around 13 seconds";
the 1-button "crashes repeatedly from Ready with a quick push of the button". Sully: "Apparently the laser didn't get fixed".
(Numbered 1.17.0, not 1.16.100: the calibration record keeps the firmware version in 8 bytes, so "1.16.100" was saved as
"1.16.10" and every new calibration would have asked to RECALIBRATE. The simulator caught it.)

HOW
1. READY keep-alive (the laser). Since 1.16.98 nothing in the firmware puts READY's dot out on a T1. On 1.16.95 the same
   2-button went dark at 13 s while the firmware's own time-out was a minute, so the MC1 module on that unit ends its pointer
   by itself (the 1-button's stays lit). Now the dot-on command is sent again every 5 s while READY sits idle (no press for
   1 s, nothing measuring, no countdown). Nothing else changes: off READY the dot rules are as before, and no other page's
   time-out moves. DIAG LASIDLE keeps= counts them. This also re-sends the dot-on after the module has finished booting,
   which should clear the dim dot Paul saw right after switch-on.
2. Crash catcher (the 1-button). Every crash Paul's unit recorded, on 1.16.94, 1.16.95 and 1.16.99, said "0x00000" with no
   call addresses. That is what the catcher wrote whenever the stack pointer was in the top 256 bytes of RAM, which is
   where an interrupt handler runs. So the crash is most likely inside an interrupt, and the catcher threw the evidence
   away. Now the frame is read wherever it is, and it also keeps the stack pointer, which interrupt was running and the
   usage-fault bits. Trip log V 4 = stack pointer, V 5 = exception number | 0x8000 (from an interrupt) | usage-fault bits
   << 16. Topside shows both as "called from 0x...": read them as these.
3. FPU interrupt (1.16.98's fix, made safe). The handler cleared the saved FPSCR at FPCAR + 0x40 even when the interrupted
   code had saved no FPU registers. FPCAR is then stale, so it cleared bits in whatever word sat there on some old stack.
   Paul's 1-button took 11,262 of these interrupts in 105 s. Now: with an FPU frame (EXC_RETURN bit 4 clear) the stacked
   FPSCR is cleared as before; without one, the live FPSCR. DIAG SLEEPIRQ noframe= counts the second kind. This is not the
   proven cause of the crash (the crashes started before 1.16.98), but it could corrupt memory.

NOT FIXED YET
The 1-button's crash itself. 1.17.0 makes the next crash name where it happened. After Paul installs it and it crashes,
his next Topside report (or events.txt from Devices\TCH-A70CD4C0) gives the interrupt number and the call addresses to
decode against elf\Tacheo_v1.17.0_cavec.elf.

TEST FOR SULLY / PAUL
1. 2-button: switch on, leave it on READY 2 minutes. The dot stays lit and bright.
2. 1-button: quick presses from READY as before. If it crashes, plug in and send a Topside report.

  time-out: 1.16.99 fails it as Paul's unit does (dark at 13 s); 1.17.0 passes 11 / 11 on cave and cavec (26 keeps in
